# VetPath social launch playbook - Frank's carousel

Everything in one place: what to upload, where, when, what to say, and
what to do in the first 90 minutes. Built from current platform research
(cited at the bottom). Square brackets are the only words Frank adds.

**SIGN-OFF GATE: the deck speaks in Frank's first-person voice. Frank
must read and approve slides 1-5 and 8 before anything posts** - they
are his story ("The day I left the military, I had no idea what came
next", "I found out the hard way", "my partner and I built it
ourselves", "don't learn it the hard way like I did"). If any line
isn't how he'd say it, send exact wording and the slide regenerates in
minutes. Optional upgrades that measurably help: his service branch
named on slide 1, and a real photo of him composited onto slide 1 or 5.

## What to upload, per platform (this matters - the formats differ)

**LinkedIn: upload `vetpath-carousel-linkedin.pdf` as a DOCUMENT post.**
LinkedIn removed native image carousels for organic posts - a PDF
document is the only swipeable format, and documents earn the
platform's dwell-time reach premium (carousels pull roughly 5x the
reach of static posts). Start a post -> "Add a document" -> upload the
PDF -> title it "The plan nobody hands you" -> paste the LinkedIn
caption below.

**Facebook: upload the 8 PNGs (vetpath-carousel-01 through 08) as ONE
multi-photo post**, in order, with the Facebook caption below. Slide 1
becomes the cover.

**Links behave differently per platform:**
- LinkedIn: an in-body link costs 40-60% of reach; a first-comment link
  costs ~5-10%. So the LinkedIn caption has NO url - Frank posts, then
  IMMEDIATELY adds the first comment below with the link.
- Facebook: link goes in the post body as written.

## When to post

- **Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday, 9-11 AM Pacific** (Frank's
  timezone is the audience's center of gravity). A 1-3 PM window is the
  runner-up. Group-post research shows peak-window posts pull ~3x the
  comments and reach of off-peak.
- Post LinkedIn and Facebook the same morning; they don't compete.
- The selfie video (script below) follows as its own post 3-5 days
  later - two touches beat one.

Both platforms boost posts that earn fast engagement, then distribute

  distribution)

## Facebook Groups - where the veteran audience actually is

Veteran life on Facebook happens in groups, not feeds. AFTER the
profile post has some engagement, Frank shares INTO groups he is
genuinely a member of - unit/base groups, veteran groups, South OC
community groups, and military-spouse groups (spouses are the share
engine for this content).

Etiquette that keeps it welcome (and un-banned):
1. Read each group's promo rules first; some require admin approval.
2. Share as his story, not an ad: two personal sentences + the link
   beats a copy-paste blast.
3. Space it out - one or two groups a day, not ten in an hour.
4. Never post into groups he just joined for the purpose.
5. If an admin objects, apologize and move on - reputation outlasts
   one post.

## Standing rules

- Never tag Camp Pendleton, the Marine Corps, the VA, or DoW pages -
  no implied endorsement, ever.
- No fabricated stats; slides and captions carry only product facts.
- The utm links are the scoreboard: the next traction snapshot ranks
  facebook vs linkedin by actual visits and signups.

## The follow-up video (3-5 days later)

A 45-60 second selfie video of Frank talking beats produced video with
this audience. Script skeleton (his words beat ours):

> "I'm [Frank], I [service background, one line]. When I left the
> military, [the one thing he wishes someone had told him]. My partner
> and I built a free website called VetPath so the next veteran doesn't
> have to learn it the hard way. Ten minutes of questions, and you walk
> out with a real plan - your benefits, your deadlines, a career
> direction, every number linked to the official source. It's free, it
> always will be, and we don't sell anything to you. It's at
> vetpathusa.com - link in the post. If you know somebody leaving the
> military this year, send it to them."

Filming: phone at eye level, outdoors or window light, captions on
(most watch muted), first take is usually the best take.
