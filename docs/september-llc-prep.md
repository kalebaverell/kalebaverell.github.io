## The DoW funding path, plain english (SBIR)

SBIR - Small Business Innovation Research - is the Department of War's
seed fund. By law it must award a slice of the research budget to small
US companies. For-profit only (the reason the nonprofit fork was
rejected). Three rungs:

1. **Phase I (~$75-150k, ~3 months).** "Prove it's feasible." Realistic
   entry: an open-topic solicitation (AFWERX runs the most accessible,
   several windows a year). VetPath's military frame: transition
   readiness and retention - every service is measured on how well its
   people land after separation.
2. **Phase II (~$750k-1.8M, ~18 months).** Build it out. Usually needs a
   named military customer who wants it.
3. **Phase III (uncapped, sole-source).** Any agency can buy without
   competition. This is the prize that justified the for-profit choice.

**What actually wins:** a military office saying "we would use this" in
writing. Frank's Camp Pendleton Transition Readiness relationship is the
future letter of support - channels first, then contracts.

**Expectations:** open-topic Phase I win rates ~10-15%. Calendar: first
application winter 2026-27, first possible dollars mid-2027. Running
costs are near zero, so the strategy survives losing rounds.

## Certifications - the ownership question (NEW, raised Aug 24)

Frank's wife is a woman veteran with a 100% disability rating. That
opens two federal doors, both potentially large:

- **SDVOSB** (Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business): federal
  sole-source and set-aside authority across agencies, plus top priority
  in VA's Vets First purchasing. Requires the company be **at least 51%
  owned AND genuinely controlled by a service-disabled veteran** -
  control means day-to-day management and the highest officer position.
  Any service-connected rating qualifies; her 100% rating more than
  clears it. (If FRANK also has a service-connected rating, he is an
  alternative path to the same cert - confirm.)
- **WOSB** (Woman-Owned Small Business): set-asides in industries where
  women are underrepresented, which typically includes software. Same
  51% ownership + control test, by a woman.
- She is both, so one genuine arrangement could qualify the company for
  BOTH certifications. Certification itself is free (SBA VetCert /
  WOSB portal), processing takes weeks to months.

**The line that cannot be crossed:** ownership and control must be REAL.
She must actually own the majority and actually run the company - not on
paper while others decide. Pass-through "rent-a-vet" arrangements are
federal crimes that are actively prosecuted, and certification reviews
look specifically for them. If the genuine intent is Frank-and-Kaleb-run,
skip the certs without regret: SBIR does not require any of them, and
they matter mostly for Phase III sole-sourcing and VA contracting later.

**Interaction check (works, if genuine):** she can be majority owner and
highest officer while Frank is the >50%-employed PI - the two rules bind
different people. All three roles need to reflect how the company truly
operates.
