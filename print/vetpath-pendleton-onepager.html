A free planning tool for Marines approaching EAS

VetPath is a free website that turns a ten-minute questionnaire into a personal 30/60/90-day gameplan: the benefits worth checking, a career direction, and the documents to gather. It was built with veterans, every number is cited to an official source, and at every claims-related step it points Marines to one place: accredited help - counselors, VSOs, and county service officers.

What Marines get

✓
A personal, printable gameplan - not a wall of links. Prioritized 30/60/90-day steps built from their answers.
✓
An EAS-date timeline: enter the date and every phase carries real calendar months - claims windows, insurance conversions, enrollment deadlines.
✓
State benefits for all 50 states and D.C., cited to official sources - including a California sheet covering CalVet programs.
✓
A career direction with real federal pay data, and how to fund the training (GI Bill, VR&E, apprenticeships).
✓
A documents checklist - DD-214, medical records, COE - so the first counselor or VSO meeting starts further ahead.

What we promise

✓
We route claims to accredited help, always. The site tells Marines that VSO and county-office help is free, and warns against paid claims consultants. We never charge for claims assistance - it is a written rule.
✓
We are not the VA, the DoW, or the Marine Corps and never determine eligibility - planning and education only, labeled that way throughout. No endorsement is implied or claimed.
✓
Free for Marines, always. The tools, the plan, and the printouts cost a service member nothing - the privacy policy explains how data is handled in plain language.
✓
Costs your office nothing. No contract, no account to manage, no data shared with us about who uses it - just a sheet on the resource table.

What it looks like at the transition office

  1. Put this sheet on the resource table or in the transition packet - the address below is all anyone needs.
  2. A Marine spends about ten minutes on their phone and walks out with a plan they can print.
  3. When the plan says "file a claim," it points to accredited help - and they arrive at that meeting with their paperwork list started.
