The Texas veteran benefits checklist

The benefits most Texas veterans have earned and many never use - each one cited to the official source so you can verify it yourself. Share it, print it, tape it to the fridge.

Federal first - the four to lock in wherever you live

VA health care - medical care, mental health, and prescriptions through the VA. Enroll even if you have other coverage.
Verify: va.gov/health-care
VA disability compensation - tax-free monthly pay for service-connected conditions. Accredited VSO help (VFW, American Legion, DAV, county service officers) is free - never pay for basic claims assistance.
Verify: va.gov/disability
GI Bill & education benefits - tuition, a housing allowance, and training support for you or, in some cases, your dependents.
Verify: va.gov/education
VA home loan - no down payment, no PMI. Start by requesting your Certificate of Eligibility.
Verify: va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans

Texas state benefits - via the Texas Veterans Commission and partner agencies

Hazlewood Act - up to 150 semester credit hours of tuition and most fees waived at Texas public colleges for qualified veterans, spouses, and dependent children; unused hours can transfer to a child through the Legacy program.
Verify: tvc.texas.gov/education/hazlewood
Disabled veteran property tax exemptions - graduated exemptions that rise with your VA rating, and a total residence homestead exemption at 100 percent or individual unemployability.
Verify: comptroller.texas.gov (search "disabled veteran exemption")
Texas Veterans Land Board loans - land, home, and home-improvement loans unique to Texas, with discounted rates for veterans rated 30 percent or more service-connected.
Verify: vlb.texas.gov
TVC veteran employment services - veteran-dedicated employment help in more than 75 Texas cities through the Jobs for Veterans State Grant.
Verify: tvc.texas.gov (official state-benefits publication)
Disabled Veteran Super Combo package - free hunting and all-water fishing license package with five endorsements for veterans rated 50 percent or more, or with loss of use of a foot or leg.
Verify: tpwd.texas.gov
Also worth knowing: Texas has no state personal income tax, so military retirement pay is not taxed by the state. The Veterans Land Board also operates Texas State Veterans Homes and free-burial Texas State Veterans Cemeteries - see glo.texas.gov/veterans.
