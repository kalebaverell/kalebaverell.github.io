How VetPath pays for itself - the honest playbook

One rule shapes everything below: free for veterans, always. That rule closes one door (charging the people we serve), and it is also our strongest asset with every funder on this list. Dollar figures are realistic placeholders drawn from published grant data and market comparables - verify each before building a budget on it.

1The entity fork comes first - it gates everything else

Profit vs. nonprofit is not a philosophy question. It is a question of which doors you want open. Each funding option below is tagged with the structure it requires.

StructureWhat it opensWhat it closesCost & timeline
501(c)(3) nonprofit NP Foundation grants, state veteran-assistance grants, most VA grant programs, tax-deductible donations, corporate giving, volunteer/AmeriCorps labor. Earned revenue (org licensing) is still allowed if mission-related. Equity investment. Founders don't own it - a board governs it. Slower to change direction. TX incorporation ~$25; IRS Form 1023-EZ $275 (small orgs) or 1023 $600. Roughly 1-6 months to determination.
For-profit (LLC / C-corp) FP DoD SBIR/STTR contracts (for-profit small businesses only), equity investment, fast decisions, founders own it. B2B revenue keeps veterans free. Nearly all foundation and state veteran-assistance grants. Tax-deductible gifts. Some partners read "for-profit" as a trust question. TX LLC ~$300, days to form. No IRS process.
Fiscal sponsorship Bridge Grant-ready in weeks: an existing 501(c)(3) receives funds on your behalf while you operate. Standard fee 5-10% of funds raised. Lets you test the nonprofit path before committing. Sponsor holds the money and some control. Not all funders accept sponsored projects. Weeks. Fee only, no formation cost.
Hybrid (both, later) Both A for-profit builds the product; an affiliated nonprofit receives grants and serves veterans. Common in this space, workable. Double the admin, real legal care needed on money flowing between the two. Not a year-one move. Add ~$2-5k/yr accounting + legal overhead.

Market reality check: of the tools on our competitive landscape, Combined Arms and PATRIOTlink are nonprofits; AI Ready Veteran is for-profit and charges veterans ~$195/yr - which is exactly the position we chose to run against.

3Option B - Charging to use the app/site Either structure

What it would look like

  • Freemium: plan free, "premium" extras $5-15/mo or ~$99-195/yr (AI Ready Veteran charges ~$195/yr).
  • One-time unlock: $29-49 for a "pro" tier.
  • Optional support: "pay it forward - fund a veteran's gameplan" donation button. Keeps the rule intact.

The honest math

  • Benchmark freemium conversion is 2-5%. At $10/mo you need ~2,000-5,000 active free users to make $5-15k/yr. We have a pilot group.
  • Most of our landscape is free (MyVetBENEFITS, Battle Buddy, PATRIOTlink, VA itself). Paid puts us on the wrong side of our own comparison table.
Recommendation: rule this out as a primary stream. "Free for veterans, always" is the wedge, the trust position, and the funder pitch. The only version worth keeping is the optional donation button and - much later, maybe - paid concierge facilitation for organizations, which is really Option D wearing a different hat.

Red lines - money we don't take, in writing: no selling or sharing veteran data; no referral fees from lenders, insurance, or paid claims consultants (the claims-shark boundary already in our compliance rules); no ad networks. Every one of these converts the trust position into inventory. The entire moat is "every number has a source and nobody is selling you anything."

7The maximum-income path (if the goal is to make as much as possible)

Everything above answers "how does it sustain itself." This section answers a different question: how do the founders make the most money. Two facts change the whole board. First, this is static software on free-tier infrastructure - gross margin on anything sold is already 85-95%, so there is no margin problem to fix, only a scale and ownership problem. Second, nonprofit assets are legally locked to the mission: a 501(c)(3) can pay salaries but never builds founder equity or exit value. If maximum income is the goal, the entity answer is for-profit, full stop - and veterans still never pay, because the free product is the customer-acquisition machine, not the merchandise.

Protecting the margin you already have

  • No paid advertising. Distribution is Frank's network, VSO partnerships, and search. Paid media is the classic margin-killer for products like this.
  • No services creep. Facilitation events sell hours at consulting margins - run them only as sales pipeline for employer and white-label deals, never as the product.

Anything that visibly monetizes individual veterans liquidates the asset for scrap value - see the red lines in section 5, which hold even under a pure profit lens. 8The five questions that decide it (for the Frank conversation)

  • 1. Do any of these paths require charging veterans? No. So the rule survives every option - that debate is closed.
  • 2. Do we ever want equity investors? Yes → for-profit or hybrid. Never → nonprofit and stop thinking about it.
  • 3. Do we want grant money within ~18 months? Yes → fiscal sponsorship this quarter, 501(c)(3) after the pilot.
  • 4. Is DoD/SBIR a serious ambition or a someday? Serious → that is the one strong argument for for-profit. Someday → it shouldn't drive the structure.
  • 5. Who owns this in ten years? Founders own an asset → for-profit. The mission owns itself → nonprofit.
The honest read of the board: the two paths now on the table are really one question - what is this for? If the goal is sustainability and maximum grant access, the funder landscape (state funds, foundations, corporate giving) tilts toward the nonprofit path, entered via fiscal sponsorship now. If the goal is maximum founder income, section 7 is the path: for-profit, employer + SBIR engines, veterans still free. Both keep the free-for-veterans rule intact, so that debate is closed either way. Decide the goal Saturday; the structure follows from it in one step.
